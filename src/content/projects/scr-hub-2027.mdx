decisions:
  - topic: Reverse protection
    options: Series Schottky (team guide default), or a P-MOSFET
    choice: P-MOSFET DMP4015SK3Q-13
    reason: At 8 A a Schottky drops about 0.45 V and dissipates about 3.6 W. The MOSFET dissipates about 0.7 W. I chose it for efficiency and less heat.
  - topic: 3.3 V supply
    options: Last year's LDO, or a buck converter
    choice: AP63301 buck
    reason: An LDO's efficiency is at most V_out / V_in = 3.3 / 12 ≈ 28% here. At the Hub's estimated 50–100 mA load, the AP63301 datasheet curve for 12 V to 3.3 V shows about 55–70%, and it accepts up to 32 V.
  - topic: Microcontroller and CAN
    options: Pico + MCP2515 (team standard), or STM32C092 + MAX3051
    choice: STM32C092 + MAX3051
    reason: One chip handles CAN, it matches the 2026 Hub and Safety Lights, and their firmware can be reused.
    status: open
  - topic: MCU clock
    options: Internal HSI48 oscillator, or an 8 MHz crystal
    choice: 8 MHz crystal, Abracon ABLS (16 pF load caps)
    reason: HSI48 can drift ±1% over temperature, and two nodes drifting apart can break CAN timing. The ABLS is ±30 ppm and has a footprint in the library.
  - topic: I²C buses
    options: One shared bus, or two buses for fault isolation
    choice: One bus
    reason: Standard for about 8 devices; firmware timeouts and bus recovery handle a stuck device.
    status: open
  - topic: Debug access
    options: None, test points only, UART header, or on-board USB-C
    choice: USB-C (CH340C) + UART header + test points
    reason: One cable for debugging, with the header as a backup and for probing.
  - topic: Layer count
    options: 2-layer, or 4-layer
    choice: 4-layer
    reason: A solid ground plane makes the buck, USB and CAN layout forgiving for a first board and frees routing space.
  - topic: Main fuse rating
    options: 10 A, or 15 A
    choice: 10 A for now
    reason: Depends on whether the NUC computer is powered through the Hub (about 2 A more).
    status: open

## Introduction

The Hub is the board every other board on our robot plugs into. It takes power from
the battery and passes three things to each board: 12 V power, the CAN bus and the
E-Stop line. For 2027, the team wanted more from it: current and voltage sensing for
every board, so we can see what each one draws. I started from last year's Hub, kept
what worked, and redesigned the power input, the 3.3 V supply, the sensing and the
debug access.

## Architecture and components

The board works in three layers. The **power layer** runs from the battery through the
input protection to a 12 V bus that feeds the seven board ports and the 3.3 V supply.
The **data layer** is the CAN bus to every port, plus an I²C bus from the
microcontroller to the seven current sensors. The **safety layer** is the E-Stop line,
which a buffer copies to every port with no firmware in the path.

- **Input protection:** an XT60 connector, a 10 A blade fuse, an SMBJ15A TVS diode that
  clamps voltage spikes, and a DMP4015SK3Q-13 P-MOSFET that blocks power if the battery
  is connected backwards.
- **3.3 V supply:** an AP63301 buck converter (3.8-32 V in, 3 A). Last year's Hub used
  an LDO, which turns the whole 12 V to 3.3 V drop into heat.
- **Board ports and current sensing:** each of the seven 6-pin Molex ports has its own
  fuse, a 20 mΩ shunt resistor and an INA220 that measures the port's current and
  voltage. At 2.5 A the shunt drops only 50 mV, and the INA220 resolves 0.5 mA.
- **Microcontroller and CAN:** an STM32C092 with a MAX3051 CAN transceiver, the same
  pair as last year's Hub and Safety Lights, so their firmware can be reused. An 8 MHz
  crystal sets the CAN timing.
- **E-Stop:** the E-Stop line comes in on one port, and an SN74AHC244 buffer drives a
  separate copy to the other six. Firmware never touches this path.
- **Debug:** USB-C through a CH340C USB-to-UART bridge, a UART header as a backup, and
  test points on the power rails, CAN, I²C and E-Stop.

## Requirements

- Power, CAN and the E-Stop line to every board through 6-pin Molex connectors.
- Current and voltage sensing for every board.
- About 5-8 A total for the boards the Hub feeds, not counting the motors.
- The E-Stop path must be hardware only, never through firmware.
- Input protection in this order: fuse, then a TVS diode, then reverse-polarity
  protection.
- A 4-layer board no bigger than 100 x 100 mm.
- Surface-mount, hand-solderable parts stocked at DigiKey or LCSC.

## Board and schematics

and a solid 12 V plane on layer 3. Each circuit block is outlined and labeled on the
silkscreen, so the top view below reads like a map of the board.
