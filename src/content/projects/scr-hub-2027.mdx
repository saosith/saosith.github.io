## Introduction

The Hub is the board every other board on our robot plugs into. It takes power from
the battery and passes three things to each board: 12 V power, the CAN bus and the
E-Stop line. For 2027, the team wanted more from it: current and voltage sensing for
every board, a temperature reading for fan control, and a measure of how busy the CAN
bus is [1]. I started from last year's Hub, reviewed what to keep, and redesigned the
power input, the 3.3 V supply, the sensing and the debug access.

**Status:** schematic complete. **Next:** finish placement and routing, then a team and
captain review before ordering. No values on this page are measured.

## Requirements

- **Distribution:** power, CAN and the E-Stop line to every board through 6-pin Molex
  connectors [1].
- **Sensing:** current and voltage for every board, plus CAN bus utilization [1].
  Temperature sensing for the fan is shared with the Fan Controller board; ownership is
  still open.
- **Load:** about 5-8 A total for the boards the Hub feeds (team estimate), not counting
  the motors.
- **Safety:** the E-Stop path must be hardware only, never through firmware [2].
- **Input protection order:** fuse, then a TVS diode in parallel, then series reverse
  protection [2].
- **Board:** 4-layer boards must stay within 100 x 100 mm [2]. Parts should be
  surface-mount, hand-solderable, and stocked at DigiKey or JLCPCB/LCSC.

## System overview

Figure 1 shows the board in three layers. The **power layer** runs from the XT60 through
the input protection to a 12 V bus that feeds the seven board ports and the 3.3 V buck.
The **data layer** is the CAN bus to every port, plus an I²C bus from the microcontroller
to the seven current sensors. The **safety layer** is the E-Stop line, which a buffer
copies to every port with no firmware in the path.

## Design

### Input protection

Battery power enters through an XT60 and passes a 10 A blade fuse in a Keystone 3557-2
holder, then an SMBJ15A TVS diode that clamps voltage spikes to ground. Normally the
team uses a series Schottky diode for reverse-polarity protection [2]. At 8 A, a
Schottky drops about 0.45 V and dissipates about 3.6 W. I used a P-MOSFET instead: the
DMP4015SK3Q-13 has 11 mΩ of on-resistance at V<sub>GS</sub> = −10 V [3], so
P = I² × R = 8² × 0.011 ≈ 0.7 W.

The drain faces the fuse and the source faces the 12 V bus, so the body diode conducts
first and the channel then turns on. If the battery is connected backwards, the gate
cannot turn on and the MOSFET blocks. A 10 kΩ resistor pulls the gate to ground, and a
12 V Zener clamps the gate-source voltage within the ±25 V rating. A 100 µF bulk
capacitor, a 10 µF ceramic and a 0.1 µF ceramic sit on the bus.

### 3.3 V supply

Last year's Hub used an LDO, which turns the whole 12 V to 3.3 V drop into heat. I
replaced it with an AP63301 synchronous buck converter (3.8-32 V input, 3 A, 500 kHz)
[4]. The component values follow the datasheet's 3.3 V table: R12 = 93.1 kΩ and
R13 = 30.1 kΩ for feedback, 56 pF feed-forward, a 4.7 µH inductor, a 0.1 µF bootstrap
capacitor, 10 µF at the input and 2 × 22 µF at the output. Because the converter is
synchronous, no catch diode is needed.

### Board ports and current sensing

Each of the seven ports has the same chain: an SMD fuse, a 20 mΩ shunt resistor, then
pin 1 of the Molex connector. Pin 6 of each connector drives a status LED through
1 kΩ. An INA220 measures the voltage across the shunt and the port voltage [6]. At
2.5 A the shunt drops 50 mV, inside the INA220's ±80 mV range, and its 10 µV step gives
10 µV / 20 mΩ = 0.5 mA resolution. The sense lines are routed as Kelvin connections
from the shunt pads. All seven sensors share one I²C bus, with unique addresses set by
strapping A0 and A1.

### Microcontroller, clock and CAN

The STM32C092 has a built-in CAN controller (FDCAN), so it needs only a MAX3051
transceiver. This matches last year's Hub and Safety Lights boards, so their firmware
can be reused. The internal HSI48 oscillator can drift ±1% over 0-85 °C [5], and two
nodes drifting in opposite directions can break CAN timing, so the CAN clock comes from
an 8 MHz crystal (Abracon ABLS-8.000MHZ-B4-T). At 125 kbps that gives
8 MHz / 125 kbps = 64 clock cycles per bit. The crystal's load capacitance is
C<sub>L</sub> = 18 pF, so the load capacitors are
C = 2 × (C<sub>L</sub> − C<sub>stray</sub>) = 2 × (18 − 10) = 16 pF [5]. Bus
termination is a 120 Ω resistor with a jumper, so the Hub can be either end of the bus.

### E-Stop path

The E-Stop line is active-low 3.3 V logic: high means the robot may run. It comes in
from the E-Stop relay board on J10, so J10's E-Stop pin is the input, and an SN74AHC244
buffer drives a separate copy to the other six ports (J4–J9). Firmware never touches this path.

### Debug access

A USB-C connector feeds a USBLC6-2SC6 ESD array and a CH340C USB-to-UART bridge, which
connects to the microcontroller's UART through 1 kΩ resistors. A 3-pin UART header sits
on the microcontroller side of those resistors, so an external adapter can still be
used. Test points cover +12 V, +3.3 V, CANH, CANL, the E-Stop line, SDA, SCL and ground.

## Layout

The board is 4-layer: signals on L1 and L4, a solid ground plane on L2, and split
+12 V / +3.3 V regions on L3, on PCBWay's 1.6 mm stack-up [7]. The design rules keep a
margin over PCBWay's minimums: 0.2 mm clearance, 0.3 mm / 0.6 mm vias, 0.5 mm copper to
the board edge, and wider minimum widths for the power nets (1.0 mm for port power,
1.5 mm for +12 V). USB D+/D− is routed as a 90 Ω differential pair.

The Molex ports sit around the edges with their port blocks (fuse, shunt, INA220) right
behind them. The XT60 input, fuse holder and P-MOSFET are on the right, the
microcontroller is in the center with the crystal at its pins, the USB-C connector is
on the bottom edge, and the buck converter is in the bottom-right corner, away from the
crystal and the USB lines. The 10 A input path is drawn as wide pours on the outer
layers.

## Open issues

- **Main fuse rating.** 10 A, or 15 A if the NUC is powered through the Hub. Waiting on
  the team's power plan.
- **Port fuse ratings.** Each board's maximum current is still needed to size its fuse.
  Port 1 uses a Littelfuse 0466005.NR while ports 2-7 use SF-1206S100-2; these should be
  reconciled.
- **Spare E-Stop buffer inputs.** Pins 15 and 17 of the SN74AHC244 are marked
  no-connect; tie them to ground.
- **Port surge protection.** The ports have fuses but no TVS diodes; decide whether each
  port needs one.
- **Overlap with the Sensor Board.** Both boards measure the main current and
  temperature; the team needs to decide which board owns what.
- **Approvals.** The STM32C092, the single I²C bus, the P-MOSFET and the PCBWay order all
  need captain approval.
