## Where this stands

This project is early. The optical side is literature study rather than my own
measurement, and the driver exists as a sizing calculation rather than a
simulated circuit. It is on the site because the analysis is real, not because
the result is finished — and the spec table above says so with empty columns
rather than filled-in guesses.

## The question

A depletion-mode micro-ring modulator is, electrically, a small reverse-biased
junction: tens of femtofarads with a series resistance set by the doping. That
is an easy load to drive slowly and a hard one to drive at 10 Gb/s, because the
driver has to swing volts across it in tens of picoseconds.

The interesting trade is that everything which makes the ring a better optical
modulator makes it a worse electrical load. Higher doping shifts more index per
volt, so the required swing falls — but it also raises junction capacitance and
lowers the RC-limited bandwidth.

## What is done

- Junction capacitance and series resistance extracted from published depletion-mode
  ring measurements, and reduced to a lumped RC the driver can be sized against.
- A first-pass CMOS inverter-chain driver sized for the resulting load, with the
  usual fan-out-of-four staging.
- An energy-per-bit estimate dominated, as expected, by C·V² in the final stage.

## What is not done

- No circuit simulation of the driver against the extracted load.
- No optical simulation of my own; the ring parameters are other people's
  measurements and are cited in the notebook.
- No thermal tuning loop, which any real ring needs — resonance drifts about
  80 pm/K and that is far wider than the channel spacing.
