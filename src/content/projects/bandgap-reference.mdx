## What it is for

Every data converter and biasing network on a chip needs a voltage that does not
move when the supply or the temperature does. A bandgap reference gets there by
adding two errors that cancel: a base–emitter voltage that falls with
temperature, and a thermal-voltage difference that rises with it.

First-order cancellation leaves a residual curvature — the sum is a parabola, not
a line. This design adds a curvature-correction branch to flatten it, which is
the difference between roughly 50 ppm/°C and roughly 20 ppm/°C.

The turnover temperature sits near 42 °C at the typical corner, deliberately
above room temperature: it places the two worst points of the curve at −40 °C
and 125 °C with roughly equal error. Moving the turnover to 27 °C would look
better on a bench at room temperature and worse everywhere else.

Across corners the reference moves about 15 mV, which is trim territory rather
than design error — absolute accuracy comes from trimming, temperature
coefficient comes from the topology.

## Layout

The PNP devices and the resistor string carry the matching burden, so both are
laid out common-centroid with dummies on all sides. The resistor string is a
single orientation — mixing orientations in a ratio you depend on is a
well-documented way to acquire a systematic error that no amount of simulation
will show you.

## Limitations and next steps

- **Line regulation is unmeasured.** The testbench exists; the sweep has not been
  run across corners.
- **No extracted-parasitic simulation** of the final layout yet.
- **No trim network.** Absolute accuracy is currently whatever the corner gives.
- **Start-up has been checked only at the typical corner.** A bandgap has a
  degenerate zero-current operating point, and the start-up circuit needs to be
  verified slow and cold, which is exactly where it is weakest.
