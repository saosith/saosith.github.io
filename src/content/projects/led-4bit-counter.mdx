## What it does

Each of the four LEDs is one bit of a binary number. I wired TI 74HC-series logic chips,
such as the SN74HC08 (AND), SN74HC32 (OR) and SN74HC04 (NOT), D flip-flops such as the
SN74HC74 to hold the current number, and a 555 timer as the clock, so the LEDs step
through a set sequence of numbers and then repeat. The blue Elegoo board in the photo only
supplies power from my laptop over USB; the 555 sets the timing.

## How it works

The 555 timer runs in astable mode, which means it has no stable state and keeps switching
on its own. A capacitor charges and discharges through resistors, and each time it crosses
a threshold the 555 flips its output between high and low. The result is a steady on/off
pulse signal, and each pulse is one clock tick.

Four D flip-flops, one per LED, hold the current 4-bit number. The gates read that number
and work out the next number in the sequence. On each clock tick, the flip-flops load the
next number, the LEDs change, and the gates start working out the one after it.
