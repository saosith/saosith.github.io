## Introduction

I wanted one microcontroller board I could reuse across projects. It runs from my
laptop's USB-C port, talks to the PC over serial and can be reprogrammed on the bench.
I designed it in KiCad around a TI MSPM0G3507 (Arm Cortex-M0+).

## Architecture and components

A USB-C charger keeps VBUS off until it sees a device, so two 5.1 kΩ resistors on CC1
and CC2 tell it the board wants power. An SPX3819 LDO then drops the 5 V to 3.3 V.

D+ and D− go through a USBLC6-2SC6 ESD array to a CH340E USB-to-UART bridge, which
shows up on the PC as a serial port.

I used an 8 MHz crystal because the internal oscillator can be off by about 1%, which
is enough to disturb UART timing. An RC filter cleans the ADC reference, since noise
on it shows up in every reading. For programming I used a TC2030 Tag-Connect
footprint, so the cable presses onto pads and no header is needed.

## Requirements

- Power from a laptop's USB-C port
- Serial link to a PC with no extra adapter
- Reprogramming on the bench with no header on the board
- Clean ADC reference
