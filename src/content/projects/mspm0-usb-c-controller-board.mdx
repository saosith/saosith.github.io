## Introduction

I wanted a general-purpose microcontroller board I could reuse across projects. I wanted it to run off my laptop's USB-C port, send data to the
PC over a serial port, and be reprogrammed on the bench without extra hardware.
So I designed this board around a TI MSPM0G3507 (Arm Cortex-M0+) [1]. It has
USB-C power, a USB-to-UART bridge, a crystal, an SWD debug pad and a filtered ADC
reference.

**Status:** schematic complete. **Next:** layout, then fabrication and bring-up.
No values on this page are measured.

## Requirements

- **Power** from a laptop's USB-C port, with no separate bench supply.
- **Serial link** to a PC for logging, with no extra adapter.
- **Reprogramming** on the bench without extra hardware on the board.
- **Clean ADC reference**, so analog measurements are not limited by reference noise.

The table at the top lists the numeric targets. They come from the USB 2.0 limits and the part datasheets; nothing is measured yet.

## Design

### Power path

USB-C supplies 5 V on VBUS. Normally, a USB-C host or charger keeps VBUS off
until it detects a device. **R5 and R6 pull CC1 and CC2 to ground through 5.1 kΩ**,
the value the USB Type-C specification sets for a sink [5], so the host sees the
board as a device requesting power. VBUS then passes through R4, a 0 Ω link that
can be replaced with a fuse or removed to cut the supply. An SPX3819 low-dropout
regulator (LDO) [4] drops the 5 V to 3.3 V and dissipates the difference as heat.

### Data path and protection

D+ and D− pass through a USBLC6-2SC6 ESD array [3] first. If someone touches the
connector, the electrostatic discharge (ESD) is clamped to ground before it reaches
the CH340E USB-to-UART bridge [2]. In layout, I'll place the array right at the
connector so the discharge has the shortest path to ground. The CH340E appears to
the PC as a standard serial port and to the microcontroller as a two-wire UART.

### Clock, reset, reference

An 8 MHz crystal with two 15 pF load capacitors drives HFXIN/HFXOUT. A crystal is
typically accurate to tens of ppm, while an on-chip RC oscillator is usually off by
around a percent, which is enough to disturb UART timing. The pull-up and capacitor
on NRST hold the microcontroller in reset until the 3.3 V rail is stable. A 100 kΩ
resistor on ROSC sets the reference current for the internal oscillator.

An RC low-pass filter sits on the ADC reference. The ADC measures every input as a
fraction of this reference voltage, so noise on the reference appears in every
reading.

Programming and debugging use a TC2030 Tag-Connect footprint: the cable's spring
pins press onto pads on the board, so no header is soldered on.

## Open issues

- **No auto-reset.** The bridge's handshake lines are not
  connected to NRST, so firmware loads through SWD and the PC cannot reset the
  microcontroller. Wiring RTS/DTR to NRST is an option for Rev B.
- **Design review before layout.** Check decoupling, the serial handshake wiring and
  the ADC reference filter against the MSPM0 [1] and CH340E [2] datasheets.
- **Crystal load capacitors.** Recalculate with C = 2 × (CL − Cstray) once the
  crystal part number is fixed.
